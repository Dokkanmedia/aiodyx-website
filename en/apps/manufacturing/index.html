From sudden line stops to planned production

Before

The production supervisor finds a missing material when the run starts, and the line waits for purchasing. Consumption is written on paper, and costs are estimated in Excel weeks later.

After

The sales order creates the manufacturing order, and MRP requests missing materials in advance. Operators start and finish operations on a tablet, and stock and cost update the moment the order closes.

End to end, step by step

  1. 1Demand creates the need

    A sales order or stock rule creates a manufacturing order for the product.

  2. 2Plan materials

    MRP reads the bill of materials, compares it with stock, then proposes purchase orders or sub-assembly orders.

  3. 3Reserve and schedule

    Materials are reserved for the order and operations are scheduled on work centres by capacity.

  4. 4Produce and inspect

    Operators run each operation from the shop-floor screen and complete mandatory quality checks at each step.

  5. 5Close and cost

    On closing, materials are consumed, the product enters stock, and the cost entry posts to accounting.

Every detail

Bills of materials

The bill of materials sets components, quantities and scrap allowance for each product. It handles product variants and sub-assemblies.

  • Multi-level bills of materials
  • Components per product variant
  • By-products and planned scrap
  • Approved versions for changes

Work orders and work centres

Each product has a routing across defined work centres, with standard times and hourly costs. You see booked capacity per centre for the coming week.

  • Standard against actual time
  • Scheduling by centre capacity
  • Alternative centres when busy
  • Work instructions on each operation

Shop-floor screens

Operators see their orders on a tablet at the machine and start, pause and record quantities and scrap. Supervisors see every work centre live.

  • Start and stop with a tap
  • Barcode scans for materials and lots
  • Downtime reasons recorded
  • Arabic interface for operators

Quality and traceability

Mandatory checkpoints block an operation until the result is recorded. A failure opens a quality alert that is tracked to closure.

  • Checks by measurement or photo
  • Inspection at goods receipt
  • Lot tracing from supplier to customer
  • Expiry dates for sensitive materials

Material planning and purchasing

MRP works out what to buy and make and when, based on orders and lead times. Suggestions become purchase orders in one click.

  • Master production schedule from forecasts
  • Lead time per supplier
  • Subcontracted manufacturing
  • Minimum and maximum stock rules

Maintenance and actual cost

Preventive maintenance is scheduled by date or running hours, and breakdowns are logged as maintenance requests. Product cost comes from actual consumption and time and is compared with standard.

  • Maintenance calendar per machine
  • Failure and repair-time indicators
  • Actual versus standard cost variance
  • Automatic cost entries in accounting

Common questions

Does it fit make-to-order and make-to-stock?

Yes. A sales order can create a manufacturing order directly, or you can rely on minimum stock rules. Both can run side by side per product.

How do we migrate our current bills of materials?

We import products, components and bills of materials from Excel or your current system, then review a sample with your production team before go-live.

Do we need special hardware on the shop floor?

A tablet or touch screen with a modern browser is enough, plus a barcode scanner if you track lots.

Does it support several plants or warehouses?

Yes. Each plant has its own warehouses and work centres, and materials move between them through documented internal transfers.

How long does a manufacturing rollout take?

We estimate 8 to 14 weeks together with inventory, purchasing and accounting. We start with one line and expand once it is stable.
